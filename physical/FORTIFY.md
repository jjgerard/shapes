# Fortifying the swivel arm

Generated by `physical/fortify.py`. Parts: `physical/hinge/fortified/`. Figure: `physical/hinge/fortified/safety.png`.

**Yes, and it needs it.** I checked every likely break point by hand calculation, using the loads from the game's own trees (HINGE.md) plus an abuse case (someone shoves or grabs the arm tip with 15 N). Several parts of the current design are under a safety factor of 1; the worst by a wide margin is the hub around the quick-release socket. The fortified parts below were built and checked on the real meshes, and the numbers recomputed.

## Where it breaks now, and after fortifying

Safety factor = allowable stress ÷ stress. Below 1 means it breaks. Allowables are assumptions for printed material under repeated loading: **PETG 20 MPa** (about half its strength), **carbon-fibre nylon 40 MPa**, and for the flexure beams a fatigue endurance of 12 MPa (PETG) and 22 MPa. They are calculations by beam theory, not tests.

| Break point | now (PETG) | fortified, PETG | fortified, carbon-fibre nylon |
|---|---|---|---|
| hub wall over the socket (under a groove), arm shoved sideways | **0.06** (358 MPa) | **1.30** (15 MPa) | **2.61** |
| hub wall over the socket (plain), arm shoved sideways | **0.19** (108 MPa) | **1.79** (11 MPa) | **3.57** |
| arm bar, in plane, shoved against the end stop | **0.56** (36 MPa) | **0.83** (24 MPa) | **1.67** |
| tongue at the slit end, shoved | **0.59** (34 MPa) | **1.67** (12 MPa) | **3.33** |
| flexure beam, every click | **0.83** (14 MPa) | **1.12** (11 MPa) | **2.05** |
| swivel stem, tilted, p90 worst x2 | **0.88** (23 MPa) | **1.32** (15 MPa) | **2.64** |

## What was weak, and what changed

**1. The hub walls around the quick-release socket (safety factor 0.06 to 0.19).** The socket takes out most of the middle of a 12 mm hub, leaving 1.9 mm of wall above and below it, and only 1.0 mm under a groove. A sideways push on the arm is carried through those thin walls as a couple. *Fix:* a longer hub (**24 mm**) and a deeper arm (**12 mm**), so the walls are about 5.9 mm (5.0 mm under a groove). The hub is a cylinder, so the extra length costs depth, not tree width.

**2. The hub is only held in by a thin ring.** While checking this I found that the arm slot is open to the floor and its opening (12.4 mm) is wider than the hub (12.0 mm), so the only thing stopping the hub dropping out of the slot was a 2.4 mm ring above the arm. *Fix:* the slot now covers only the arm's own height, leaving a **full ring 6.4 mm below and 6.4 mm above** the arm. They hold the hub in and carry the sideways load. (Measured: pulling the hub down the slot, the housing now blocks it at once, overlap 23 mm³ at 0.5 mm, 87 mm³ at 1 mm, 209 mm³ at 2 mm.)

**3. The arm against its end stop, in plane (0.56).** If the arm is pushed past ±55° the arm's edge hits the slot wall with the whole arm as a lever. A thicker arm helps (the section modulus goes up 1.5×); the rest has to come from the material. This is the one that carbon-fibre nylon fixes and PETG does not. **Recommendation: print arms and stems in carbon-fibre nylon, or glue a Ø3 mm steel or carbon rod through the arm's length** (that adds strength for the cost of a hole; I have not calculated how much).

**4. The slit end in the tongue (0.59).** A square corner at the end of the slit concentrates the stress about three times. *Fix:* a full round at the slit end (concentration about 1.6).

**5. The flexure beams (0.83 against fatigue).** They are bent every click. *Fix:* longer beams (16.5 mm instead of 12.5) with a thicker section (2.6 mm instead of 2.0), which gives the same stiffness (16.1 N/mm each) at a stress about 25% lower, and rounded slot ends where the beam joins the lid. The peak detent torque stays at 104 / 220 / 382 N·mm (was 109 / 230 / 400).

**6. The swivel stem (0.88 tilted).** The stem at the arm tip carries the child's whole subtree. *Fix:* 12 mm deep like the arm, and it **widens beyond the housing** (5 mm at the hub, 10 mm from 18 mm out) with straight tapers, rather than a bar. It must stay 5 mm wide inside the housing slot or it would hit the slot at ±50°.

**7. The arm bar bending out of plane (0.89 shoved, 1.16 carried).** The 12 mm depth fixes it (section modulus ×2.25).

**8. The hanger hole in the test arm (a Ø4 hole in a 5 mm bar leaves 0.5 mm ligaments).** The test arm now has a flared boss (10 mm wide) round it. A production arm has no hole.

## What the script checked on the real meshes

- All parts are watertight solids (7 of them).
- With the longer hub and deeper arm, **the arm still turns freely through ±55°** (overlap 0.000 mm³) and touches at ±60°.
- The swivel hub with its stem and the real plug turns through ±50° without touching the housing (overlap 0.000 mm³).
- Pushing the arm in, the barbs interfere by 2.58 mm³ (predicted 2.73); seated, nothing interferes; it pulls out clean at −50°, 0° and +50°.

## What is not fixed, or not known

- **A hard shove can still break a PETG arm.** The one case PETG cannot cover is the in-plane shove against the end stop (safety factor under 1). Either use carbon-fibre nylon or a rod, or accept that arms are the consumable part: they are the same part everywhere and snap in and out.
- **Printing matters as much as geometry.** Arms and stems should be printed lying flat so the layers run along them (never standing up), with at least four perimeters; the hub with its axis vertical. Layer adhesion is the usual first failure.
- **A fuse.** The best protection is a part that fails first and is cheap to replace. Here the arm is that part, which is why it should snap out and not be glued in.
- **The numbers are calculated.** The load cases (15 N shove, doubling for handling) and the material allowables are my assumptions. Printing the three arm variants and loading them is the real test; the document before this (STOW.md) says how.
- **The swivel is still only a concept** (ARM_OPTIONS.md): this builds its carrier hub and stem, not the whole tip cartridge, and the layout test says it makes trees 20 to 30% taller.
