# Arm layout

Generated by `physical/arms.py` from the game's own trees (`data.js`). Drawings: `physical/out/arms_piece.svg` (one piece) and `physical/out/arms_trees.svg` (trees built with these arms).

## The design

- **Two- and three-slot pieces** (TP, VP, NP, PP, CP, and every bar): each slot has a hinged arm, **80 mm long**, 5 mm wide, that **clicks at 0°, ±25° and ±50°** from straight down. **No sliding part.**
- **Single-slot pieces** (DP, N′, AdjP, AdvP): a fixed 22 mm stub, straight down, no hinge. They only ever have one child, so they never need to reach sideways.
- **Hinges** sit on the bottom of the body 16 mm apart; a hinge hub is 14 mm wide. Body size: three-slot 54 × 26 mm, two-slot 38 mm, one-slot and heads 30 mm (the socket cup is 27.9 mm across).
- **Layout rule:** when it is built, the parent sits centred over its children, and every arm is set to the nearest click; no two parts of different branches may come within 5 mm.

## Why these numbers

Every Level 1 target and Level 2 sentence in both modes (14 trees, 233 nodes) was laid out in front view with real piece sizes, with the arms restricted to a set of click settings, and the smallest set that builds all of them was kept.

| Arm design | Trees that can be built | Biggest flat tree | Biggest X-bar sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed 64 mm, hinge 0 / ±30 / ±60 | all 14 | 357 × 337 mm | 496 × 875 mm |
| fixed 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±30 / ±60 | all 14 | 387 × 363 mm | 540 × 936 mm |
| fixed 80 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50  (chosen) | all 14 | 376 × 406 mm | 527 × 1051 mm |
| fixed 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50  (the earlier design) | **12 of 14** | 350 × 379 mm | — |
| fixed 66 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50 / ±70 | all 14 | 331 × 358 mm | 472 × 914 mm |
| telescoping 44 or 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50 | all 14 | 318 × 288 mm | 420 × 786 mm |
| telescoping 44 / 58 / 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50 | all 14 | 293 × 304 mm | 420 × 786 mm |

Findings:
- The reach needed is modest because **the parent can sit centred over its children**. My first attempt, which hung children from one side, needed arms over a metre long.
- A fixed arm is enough. The telescoping rows show what a sliding arm would buy; it adds a sliding part, which is the most likely thing to wobble or snap, and the arm must overlap its sleeve by at least 10 mm. I would only add it if the biggest trees turn out to be too big for your tables.
- Not every set of clicks works: see the "Trees that can be built" column. The hinge design (HINGE.md) set the hinge pitch and arm width; with those, the 72 mm arm of the earlier design no longer builds the biggest X-bar tree, and an 80 mm arm does. Shorter arms (64 to 72 mm) also build every tree with clicks at 0 / ±30° / ±60°, but the housing cannot swing an arm that far and still hold the hub in (HINGE.md), so I kept ±50° and the longer arm.

## Things this does not settle

- **Big X-bar trees are big.** The Level 1 target (37 pieces) and the question sentences are about 0.4 m wide and 0.65 to 1 m tall. They are fine on a floor or a long table, and impractical to lift whole. Single phrases (a DP, a VP) are small enough to pick up.
- **Unused arms must stow.** The three-slot pieces are often used with one or two arms (NP uses only one of its three 16 times in the game's trees; TP leaves its middle arm empty in every X-bar tree). A plug is 22.7 mm wide and hinges are 13 mm apart, so a dangling arm would hit its neighbour. Options: fold the arm up flat against the back of the body (the body would need to be about 40 mm deep), or make arms removable on a keyed hub. I have not designed this yet.
- **The hinge has to hold a hanging phrase.** HINGE.md works out the torque from these trees and designs a detent hinge for it.
- **Arm strength.** The 5 × 9 mm arm carries the load comfortably in bending; the hinge and the plug joint are the weak points, not the arm.
