# Arm layout

Generated by `physical/arms.py` from the game's own trees (`data.js`). Drawings: `physical/out/arms_piece.svg` (one piece) and `physical/out/arms_trees.svg` (trees built with these arms).

## The design

- **Two- and three-slot pieces** (TP, VP, NP, PP, CP, and every bar): each slot has a hinged arm, **72 mm long**, 7 × 7 mm in section, that **clicks at 0°, ±25° and ±50°** from straight down. **No sliding part.**
- **Single-slot pieces** (DP, N′, AdjP, AdvP): a fixed 22 mm stub, straight down, no hinge. They only ever have one child, so they never need to reach sideways.
- **Hinges** sit on the bottom of the body 13 mm apart; a hinge hub is 12 mm wide. Body size: three-slot 46 × 26 mm, two-slot 33 mm, one-slot and heads 30 mm (the socket cup is 27.9 mm across).
- **Layout rule:** when it is built, the parent sits centred over its children, and every arm is set to the nearest click; no two parts of different branches may come within 5 mm.

## Why these numbers

Every Level 1 target and Level 2 sentence in both modes (14 trees, 233 nodes) was laid out in front view with real piece sizes, with the arms restricted to a set of click settings, and the smallest set that builds all of them was kept.

| Arm design | Trees that can be built | Biggest flat tree | Biggest X-bar sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed 64 mm, hinge 0 / ±30 / ±60 | **12 of 14** | 340 × 337 mm | — |
| fixed 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±30 / ±60 | **12 of 14** | 370 × 363 mm | — |
| fixed 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50  (chosen) | all 14 | 334 × 379 mm | 465 × 984 mm |
| fixed 66 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50 / ±70 | all 14 | 314 × 358 mm | 448 × 914 mm |
| telescoping 44 or 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50 | all 14 | 316 × 288 mm | 396 × 786 mm |
| telescoping 44 / 58 / 72 mm, hinge 0 / ±25 / ±50 | all 14 | 277 × 304 mm | 385 × 786 mm |

Findings:
- The reach needed is modest (at most about 45 mm sideways) **because the parent can sit centred over its children**. My first attempt, which hung children from one side, needed arms over a metre long.
- A fixed arm is enough. Telescoping (44 or 72 mm) shrinks the biggest trees by roughly 15 to 20 percent, but adds a sliding part, which is the most likely thing to wobble or snap, and the arm must overlap its sleeve by at least 14 mm, so a telescoping arm's longest length cannot exceed twice its shortest minus 14 mm. I would only add it if the biggest trees turn out to be too big for your tables.
- Not every set of clicks works: with the short single-slot stubs, 0 / ±30° / ±60° fails on two trees at 64 mm and at 72 mm, and adding ±70° at 66 mm works but needs a seventh click.

## Things this does not settle

- **Big X-bar trees are big.** The Level 1 target (37 pieces) and the question sentences are about 0.4 m wide and 0.65 to 1 m tall. They are fine on a floor or a long table, and impractical to lift whole. Single phrases (a DP, a VP) are small enough to pick up.
- **Unused arms must stow.** The three-slot pieces are often used with one or two arms (NP uses only one of its three 16 times in the game's trees; TP leaves its middle arm empty in every X-bar tree). A plug is 22.7 mm wide and hinges are 13 mm apart, so a dangling arm would hit its neighbour. Options: fold the arm up flat against the back of the body (the body would need to be about 40 mm deep), or make arms removable on a keyed hub. I have not designed this yet.
- **The hinge has to hold a hanging phrase.** A phrase of about 150 g on a 72 mm arm at 50° is roughly 80 to 110 N·mm at the hinge. A single printed click will not hold that; the detent needs real teeth or a clamp. This is the next thing to prototype.
- **Arm strength.** A 7 × 7 mm arm carries that load comfortably in bending (about 2 MPa); the hinge and the plug joint are the weak points, not the arm.
