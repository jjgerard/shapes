# The sliding arm

Generated by `physical/slide.py`. Parts: `physical/hinge/slide/`. Figures: `sections.png`, `cross.png`, `parts.png`, `trees.png` in the same folder.

The swinging arm needed a hinge cartridge and a swivel cartridge on every arm (`SWIVEL.md`): about 34 g per arm, 16 screws, and the biggest X′ tree still could not be built. This replaces both with an arm that slides sideways, so the child hangs straight down at every setting and nothing has to keep it upright.

## Which kind of slide

`ARM_OPTIONS.md` found that a sliding arm builds all 10 of the game's trees, but only drew the beam out to the carriage. A real slide has a part that stays behind, so I measured three real outlines with the same layout test (arm 15 mm tall, 72 mm reach, child 34 mm below the body):

| Slide | Trees built | Widest tree | Tallest tree |
|---|---|---|---|
| beam drawn only out to the carriage (the earlier test) | **10 of 10** | 510 mm | 722 mm |
| fixed rail that stays out at full reach | **3 of 10** | 330 mm | 242 mm |
| single bar that slides, with its tail | **7 of 10** | 518 mm | 602 mm |
| two-stage telescope (this design) | **10 of 10** | 519 mm | 722 mm |

A rail that stays out at full reach pokes into its neighbours at its own height in 7 of the 10 trees; a single bar with a tail that sticks out the far side fails 3. **A two-stage telescope** keeps everything that is not needed inside the body's width (all but its inboard end, which sits under the body), and builds all ten, with 0 problems found by an independent distance check of the finished layouts.

## How it works

Each moving arm is **four printed parts and no screws**. A two-slot or three-slot piece has two moving arms (left and right), turned 180° from each other so one set of parts makes both; a three-slot piece's middle slot is a fixed post.

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| `slide_A.stl` | the **fixed bar**. It has a dovetail along its top that slides into a groove under the body *from the outer side*, and a latch beam that clicks into a dimple; to take an arm off, pull it out sideways (about 6 N). Underneath, a dovetail tongue for S1 |
| `slide_S1.stl` | the **middle slider**: a groove on top for A's tongue, a tongue underneath for S2, and a detent beam |
| `slide_S2.stl` | the **outer slider**: a groove on top for S1's tongue, a detent beam, and the **foot**, a block that jogs sideways to the piece's centre plane and holds the plug insert |
| `slide_insert.stl` | the **keyed plug** on a round shank that snaps into the foot (here the key for a DP). The insert is the only key-specific part (its snap-in retention is not analysed), so a kit needs 26 inserts, not 26 arms |
| `slide_body_stub.stl` | *reference only*: what the underside of a three-slot body needs (the two dovetail grooves and the two latch dimples). The body itself is not designed yet |

Dimensions: each slider is 14 mm wide and 48 mm long with a 6 mm base; the dovetail has a 3.0 mm neck, 5.8 mm head and is 1.4 mm deep, with 0.25 mm clearance; the three bases together stack 19 mm below the body. Each stage has **two stops, in and out (36 mm apart)**, so the child hangs **0, 36 or 72 mm** from its slot. The plug's base face is 34 mm below the body.

![across the piece](cross.png)

The left and right stacks sit side by side in z (their centre lines 21 mm apart), with the fixed middle post between them, so **every two- and three-slot body is 35 mm thick** (heads and one-slot pieces can stay at 24 mm: the plug is always at the centre plane).

![parts](parts.png)

**Detent.** Each stage has a printed flexure beam in the strip beside the dovetail: a 14 mm cantilever 2.0 × 2.4 mm with a 0.7 mm bump with 60° flanks that drops into a dimple at *in* and at *out*. Retracting stops against the closed end of the groove; the *out* dimple has a steeper far flank (70°) that acts as the stop. The force to push a stage out of a stop is **2.6 / 6.4 / 15.0 N** for friction 0 / 0.3 / 0.45; the stop on the far side is about 26 N at friction 0.3. The beam's bending stress when it rides over the bump is 11 MPa, right at the repeated-flexing limit I used for PETG (12 MPa), so it should be treated as a wear part.

## What the script checked on the real meshes

- All four parts are watertight solids.
- **16 combinations of settings** for the right and left arm of a three-slot piece (each stage in or out, both arms): no part overlaps any other, the body, the middle post, or the child's block hanging below the plug (largest overlap 0.000 mm³).
- The plug ends up **0, 36 and 72 mm** from rest (measured on the meshes), and the plug always points straight down.
- **Detents:** seated at every stop with no overlap, and pressing in with 1.14 mm³ half-way between the stops, so the bump really does ride on the face. The arm's mount latch behaves the same way.

## Loads

The weights come from the same 10 trees laid out with this arm, with real masses: one arm 12.7 g (A 2.8, S1 2.3, S2 with foot and plug 7.6), a three-slot piece 37 g and a two-slot piece 35 g, at 50% fill in PETG. The weight hanging from one of the 108 moving arms: median 53 g, 90th percentile 231 g, largest 640 g. Stress is at the weakest section of the stage that carries most (S1 where A's tongue ends), at full extension:

| Hanging weight | S1 bending | S2 bending | Tongue shear | Dovetail twist (from the foot's 10.5 mm offset) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| median (53 g) | 0.7 MPa (safety 30.6) | 0.4 MPa (safety 56.9) | 0.2 MPa (safety 61.7) | 1.0 N per flank |
| 90% (231 g) | 2.5 MPa (safety 8.0) | 1.3 MPa (safety 14.9) | 0.7 MPa (safety 16.2) | 3.9 N per flank |
| max (640 g) | 6.7 MPa (safety 3.0) | 3.6 MPa (safety 5.5) | 2.0 MPa (safety 6.0) | 10.6 N per flank |
| abuse (1529 g) | 15.8 MPa (safety 1.3) | 8.5 MPa (safety 2.4) | 4.7 MPa (safety 2.6) | 25.0 N per flank |

(The *abuse* row is a hand pulling down 15 N on the carriage, the same shove used in `FORTIFY.md`.) A base of 5 mm gave a safety factor of 0.9 at that shove, so **the layers are 6 mm** and the stack is 1 mm taller; the safety factor is now **1.3**, still the first place I would look for a break.

**Holding.** Along the slide, a stage has to resist the weight of everything below it only if the slide is tilted. With the slide pointing *straight down* (the worst case, which a phrase held by its top piece never reaches), a stage holds **92% / 100% / 100%** of the arms at friction 0 / 0.3 / 0.45. At friction 0.3 the heaviest arm is only just inside the limit (6.39 N push-out against 6.38 N). **Friction is the number I cannot calculate**: it is the first thing to measure on a printed coupon.

## What this does not settle

- **Nothing has been printed.** The clearances (0.25 mm), the 0.4 mm detent engagement and the friction are all assumptions; the first print should be a short coupon of A, S1 and S2 at 1/3 length to tune them.
- **The body is not designed.** `slide_body_stub.stl` gives only the underside. The body must also hold the sockets, be 35 mm thick, and be strong at the two grooves (they are 1.6 mm deep and sit in a 26 mm tall block).
- **Print orientation** (a suggestion): each slider lying on one of its 14 mm faces, so the dovetail flanks are 45° overhangs, the layer lines run along the beam and the flexure beams bend within a layer.
- The model of the loads ignores dynamic effects (dropping a phrase) and a real shove sideways (z); the foot's jog gives a twist on the dovetail that is small but not checked on a print.
- A 2-slot piece's A bars reach 13 mm past the body's edge (the layout test includes this).
- The foot is solid and heavy (7.6 g with the plug); a ribbed foot would save about 2 g per arm.
