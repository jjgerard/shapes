# The swivel tip cartridge

Generated by `physical/swivel.py`. Parts: `physical/hinge/swivel/`. Figure: `physical/hinge/swivel/kinematics.png`.

The cartridge sits on the end of each hinged arm and keeps the child it carries plumb, so that the layouts in `ARMS.md` (which assume upright children) are true. It is the hinge cartridge again, turned round, with the fortified dimensions from `FORTIFY.md`.

## The parts

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| `swivel_arm_tip.stl` | the arm bar with a snap-in tongue at the hinge end, a taper, and the **tip housing** at the other end, all one print. The housing is 25 × 33 × 26 mm |
| `swivel_swivel_hub.stl` | the **swivel hub**: a Ø12 × 24 mm hub with five detent grooves on each end, a 12 mm deep stem that widens beyond the housing, and the kit's real keyed plug (the key for a DP here). **The plug is the key-specific part: each slot type needs its own hub**, so a kit has as many hubs as it has keys (26) |
| `swivel_lid_t21/t26/t31.stl` | the flexure lids, three thicknesses to find the right hold; **two of the same thickness per cartridge**, printed plate down |
| screws | four M3 per lid, eight per cartridge (twelve per arm with the hinge) |

Printed in PETG at about half fill, a cartridge weighs roughly **21 g** (arm with housing 13, hub 5, lids 2). The loads in `HINGE.md` assumed about 2 g for an arm; **with the real masses the loads are about three times higher (see "The weight problem" below).**

## How it works

The housing is rigid with the arm and the hub turns in it, clicking at the same five angles as the hinge (0°, ±25°, ±50°). The arm's own click is α; turn the swivel to −α and the two cancel, so the stem points straight down. In practice: set the arm, then twist the child until it clicks upright. If you do not, the child tilts with the arm (mismatched at +50°, the stem tilts +50°).

## The weight problem

The earlier load numbers (`HINGE.md`, `ARM_OPTIONS.md`) assumed a piece weighs 8 to 19 g with about 2 g per arm. The real parts are heavier: a hinge cartridge (housing, hub and lids) is about **13 g**, every slot of a piece has one, and every used arm carries about **21 g** at its tip. A three-slot piece is about 50 g before any arm. Recomputed with those masses (same trees, same upright layouts; masses are PETG at about half fill, so treat as estimates):

| Hinge torque (N·mm) | median | 75% | 90% | largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hanging, before | 6 | 31 | 134 | 613 |
| **hanging, now** | **13** | **99** | **455** | **2156** |
| worst tilt, before | 20 | 139 | 460 | 1949 |
| **worst tilt, now** | **36** | **397** | **1488** | **6526** |
| swivel tip, net torque, now | 0 | 26 | 137 | 1414 |

The loads are about **3.2×** what they were at the 90th percentile. The hinge detent design (peak torque 109 / 230 / 400 N·mm without / with friction) now holds 88% of arms hanging as built and 69% in a worst-case tilt (at μ 0.3), against 96% and 83% before; the swivel detent holds 93% of children upright. The "carried" rows in `FORTIFY.md` scale the same way: their safety factors fall by about 3.2×, so the arm bar carried out of plane (2.6) is about 0.8 and the stem hanging (2.5) about 0.8. The shoved rows do not change (they are set by a hand, not by the structure).

**What this means.** The system carries a lot of its own weight, and almost all of it is the cartridges. Either the cartridges get much lighter (a compact snap-lid version with thin walls and less fill), or the detents get stronger (the 2.4 mm flexure, with a stiffer feel), or the arms are made to take the load, or trees are not lifted whole. This is the strongest argument yet for the sliding arm (`ARM_OPTIONS.md`), which has no cartridge at all.

## What this does not settle

- **The biggest X′ tree** does not build with the cartridge at either arm length, and every other tree gets 18 to 28% taller. The cartridge is large (it is a screwed hinge, with ears for the screws); a compact snap-lid version would be smaller but is not designed.
- **Assembly.** Eight screws per cartridge, sixteen with the hinge, per arm. A kit of 28 arms is hundreds of screws. The sliding arm (`ARM_OPTIONS.md`) has none of this.
- **Keys.** The plug is part of the swivel hub, so changing which slot an arm serves means swapping the hub (a lid has to come off). A key module that snaps onto a common stem would make arms universal; not designed.
- **Strength.** The stem, housing and flexures use the fortified dimensions (`FORTIFY.md`); the joint where the arm bar meets the tip housing is a taper over 17 mm and has not been analysed separately.
- **Print resolution and the detent.** As for the hinge: the groove and wedge are 2 to 3 line widths on a 0.4 mm nozzle.
- **Nothing here has been printed.** The numbers are geometry and calculation.
