# How should the arm move?

Generated by `physical/arm_options.py` from the game's own trees. Figure: `physical/out/arm_options.png`.

The swinging arm has a problem (STOW.md): a child on a rigid plug at the arm's tip tilts with the arm, and the trees sprawl. I was asked to design a **swivel at the arm tip** to keep children upright. Before building it I checked what it would cost, and compared it with an arm that **slides sideways** instead, since a slide cannot tilt anything.

## The swivel

A swivel is only worth it if it can hold the child upright against the torque of its own subtree. Torque of everything hanging under each of the 116 hinged arms, about the arm-tip pivot, hanging upright (piece masses are the same assumption as HINGE.md):

| median | 75% of arms | 90% of arms | largest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 N·mm | 9 | 45 | 402 |

The three worst are the top arms of the biggest trees: CP in "xbar:L1 one-more-branch" (346 g, 402 N·mm), C′ in "xbar:L1 one-more-branch" (325 g, 272 N·mm), CP in "xbar:L2 did-the-cat-chase-the-mouse" (233 g, 196 N·mm). A detent holding 110 N·mm would keep 94% of children upright, and 230 N·mm 98%. That is exactly the range of the hinge detent (HINGE.md), so the swivel would be **the same cartridge again**, with a stem and the plug in place of the arm.

That works mechanically, but it is heavy. A cartridge (about 25 mm wide, 28 mm of stem) on the tip of every hinged arm, laid out with the same arms:

And there is still a second setting to make: when the arm is moved, the child has to be twisted back upright to the matching click. So the swivel works, but at a real price.

## The sliding arm

Here the child moves sideways and hangs straight down from a stem. Nothing tilts, so nothing needs to keep it upright. Left arms slide left and right arms slide right; the **middle arm of a three-slot piece does not move at all**. Each moving arm has four positions: 0, 24, 48 and 72 mm out. The child hangs 24 mm below the body.

It builds **10 of 10** trees, and the independent distance check found 0 problems. The trees are also much smaller than with swinging arms (the biggest X′ tree is 486 × 622 mm, against 527 × 1051 mm). And **unused arms can stay on** (last column): with 10 of 10 trees building, the stowing problem mostly goes away too. I also tried smaller sets: three positions (0, 36, 72 mm) still build everything if the middle arm can move one step either way; two positions (0, 72) do not.

## What the sliding model leaves out

This is a layout test, not a mechanism, and it is simpler than reality in ways that matter:

- **The beam's tail.** A bar that slides out one side has to stay engaged in the body, so a bar reaching 72 mm needs about 85 mm of length, and when retracted its tail pokes out the other side. The test only draws the beam out to the carriage. **This is now settled in `SLIDE.md`**: a fixed rail builds 3 of the 10 trees, a bar with a tail 7, and a two-stage telescope all 10.
- **Beams of neighbouring pieces.** Beams are only checked against bodies and stems, not against each other.
- **The 24 mm drop** between body and child is assumed, not designed.
- **The detent.** A sliding arm only has to resist a few newtons along the slide (the weight of a hanging phrase tilted on its side), instead of a hundred N·mm of torque, so it should be much easier to print than the hinge, but it is not designed.
- **Load on the beam.** The weight hangs off the end of a cantilever; at 72 mm out, 350 g is about 250 N·mm of bending, which a bar of this size carries, and the guide in the body has to resist it as a couple.

## Where this leaves the earlier work

- The **swinging arm and its hinge** (ARMS.md, HINGE.md, STOW.md) is a worked, checked design, but it needs the swivel to be correct, and the swivel is costly.
- The **sliding arm** is designed in `SLIDE.md`: a two-stage telescoping dovetail slide, 4 printed parts and no screws, which builds all 10 trees.
- The **keys and the quick-release idea** (SPOKES.md) are unaffected either way.
