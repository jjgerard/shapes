## What it has to hold

Computed from the game's own trees (the 10 distinct ones), laid out with the arm design (80 mm arms, 16 mm hinge pitch, 5 mm-wide arms), by adding up the weight of everything that hangs from each of the 116 hinged arms. Piece masses are **assumed** (heads 8 g, one-slot 9 g, two-slot 13 g, three-slot 19 g, printed in PETG at about 30% effective fill); the torques scale directly with that.

| Torque on a hinge (N·mm) | median | 75% of arms | 90% of arms | largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hanging as built | 6 | 31 | 134 | 613 |
| worst tilt (held sideways) | 20 | 139 | 460 | 1949 |

The load is very uneven: most arms carry almost nothing, and a handful at the top of the biggest trees carry a lot. The three heaviest:
- CP in "xbar:L1 one-more-branch": 348 g hanging, 1949 N·mm worst tilt
- C′ in "xbar:L1 one-more-branch": 327 g hanging, 1632 N·mm worst tilt
- TP in "xbar:L1 one-more-branch": 242 g hanging, 1165 N·mm worst tilt

**Target: hold 250 N·mm, and let a hand overcome it (under 500 N·mm, which is about 6 N at the arm tip).** That is a firm hinge, not a loose one. Trees laid flat on a table put no gravity load on their hinges at all; the load only matters when a phrase is lifted.

## The mechanism

A **face detent**: five V-grooves on each end face of the hub (at the five clicks), and on each end a **wedge on a flexure plate** pressing on the face. Seated in a groove the arm is locked; to turn it the hub has to push the plate back by 0.5 mm, which takes torque. Between the clicks the wedge rides on the flat face and the arm is held only by light friction.

```
   top lid     ======= beam =======      <- wedge presses on the hub face
   hub         = = = grooves = = =
   bottom lid  ======= beam =======
   housing: a block with a bore for the hub and a slot for the arm to swing in
```

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| hub and arm | hub Ø12 × 12 mm with the arm 5 × 9 mm, 80 mm from the axis; 5 grooves on each hub face; a Ø4 hole near the arm tip for hanging weights in tests |
| housing | block with a Ø12.5 bore (0.25 mm clearance), a slot 164° wide for the arm, and four M3 screw holes |
| two lids | identical plates, one on each end, each with a flexure beam and wedge; screwed to the housing (the real piece would use snap hooks) |

Why these numbers: the hinges are 16 mm apart because a 12 mm hub in a 1 mm wall needs that much room for a neighbour's arm to swing under it at 50° (measured clearance **0.53 mm**). The arm is only 5 mm wide, because a wider arm's edges sweep so far near the hub that the slot would have to open to 180° and the hub would no longer be held (the narrow arm's edge sweeps 24.6° wider than its centre line; the slot has **7.4°** of margin beyond ±50°). Both changes were fed back into the layout: all 10 distinct game trees still build.

## Detent torque

The torque is set by the flexure stiffness, how far the wedge has to be pushed (δ = 0.5 mm), the ring radius (4.5 mm) and the flank angle (55°). The peak torque to leave a click (both flexures) is `2 k δ r tan(flank)` without friction, and `(tan + μ)/(1 − μ tan)` times that with it.

| Flexure thickness | stiffness each | stress at full push | strain | peak torque, no friction | μ = 0.3 | μ = 0.45 | friction between clicks (μ 0.3) | arms held, no friction (hanging / worst tilt) | arms held at μ 0.3 (hanging / worst tilt) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.6 mm | 8.7 N/mm | 11.5 MPa | 0.8% | 56 N·mm | 118 | 205 | 12 N·mm | 85% / 67% | 90% / 72% |
| 2.0 mm **(design)** | 16.9 N/mm | 14.4 MPa | 1.0% | 109 N·mm | 230 | 400 | 23 N·mm | 90% / 70% | 96% / 83% |
| 2.4 mm | 29.2 N/mm | 17.3 MPa | 1.2% | 188 N·mm | 397 | 691 | 39 N·mm | 94% / 81% | 98% / 90% |

These are **calculated, not measured**. They assume printed PETG with a stiffness of 1500 N/mm² (±30%) and a friction coefficient between 0.15 and 0.45, so the real hold is a range. The **2.0 mm flexure is the design point**: it holds 109 N·mm even with no friction, about 230 N·mm at a typical μ of 0.3 (right on the 250 N·mm target), and still releases under 500 N·mm (400 N·mm at μ 0.45). The thicker flexure is stiffer than a hand should have to overcome if the surfaces are grippy, and the thinner one is the fallback if they are. The stress is under 1.5% strain at every thickness.

## What the script checked on the real meshes

- All parts are watertight solids.
- **The hub turns freely through ±55°** without touching the housing or the lid plates (largest overlap 0.000 mm³), and it does touch at ±60°, so the check can see a collision.
- **The detent interference measured in 3D matches the profile model** (correlation 1.000 over 121 angles; the worst error on a land is 1%). The wedge sits in each groove with no interference (zero play), and rides up onto the flat face between clicks.

## Print and test it

Print the housing and the hub and arm as modelled (axis vertical, arm flat on the bed), and **two lids of the same thickness** (printed plate down, wedge up). The bottom lid is the same part as the top lid, turned over. You need four M3 screws and nuts (20 to 25 mm long) per hinge. PETG is recommended, 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters, 40% or more infill in the arm. The grooves on the hub's bottom face sit against the bed, so turn on elephant-foot compensation or check that they came out clean. The slot ceiling in the housing bridges a wide gap, so use supports there or accept some sag.

Test one lid thickness at a time (1.6, 2.0, 2.4 mm):
1. Assemble, and turn the arm by hand through all five clicks. Does each click feel distinct, and does the arm hold between them?
2. **Measure the hold.** Put the housing on its side so the arm points horizontally at the 0° click (use the arm's hole at 73 mm from the axis). Hang a bag from the hole and add weight until the arm drops out of the click. The torque is `mass (kg) × 9.81 × 73 mm`; 250 N·mm is about 350 g.
3. Repeat after 50 turns of the arm through all the clicks, to see whether the flexures wear or the hold fades.

## What this does not settle

- **Real hold.** The numbers above are a prediction; the test in the previous section is what decides the thickness.
- **Assembly.** The coupon uses screws. The real piece needs a snap-on lid (or one lid carrying all three flexures) and a way for the hub to go in; and it must be strong enough that a student cannot pull the arm off.
- **Unused arms.** The hinge turns ±50° and the slot opens ±82°, so a folded-back arm is not possible. See STOW.md for what to do with them (arms that snap in and come off).
- **Wear.** Printed flexures that are bent 0.5 mm every click will creep over time; the 50-cycle test in step 3 is a first look.
- **Print resolution.** The groove is 1.2 mm wide and 0.86 mm deep, and the wedge tip is 0.5 mm; on a 0.4 mm nozzle that is two to three line widths. It may need a finer layer height or a slightly larger groove.
